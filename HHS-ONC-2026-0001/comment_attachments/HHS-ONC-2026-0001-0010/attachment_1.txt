Subject: A Scalable Protocol for Deflationary AI Governance and Program Integrity
Dear Deputy Secretary O’Neill and Dr. Mandl,
I am submitting the enclosed comment in response to HHS’s Request for Information on
accelerating the adoption and use of artificial intelligence in clinical care. The core
challenge HHS faces is not whether AI can be deployed, but how to scale its use without
expanding federal operational burden or relying on opaque, non-scalable model-level
oversight.
The enclosed comment describes a scalable, non-device AI governance protocol that
enables HHS to accelerate adoption by aligning semantic responsibility with carriers,
providers, and intermediaries—where existing accuracy obligations already reside—while
preserving federal oversight through deterministic, audit-ready outputs. This approach
operationalizes the “AI-enabling infrastructure and documentation” principles articulated
in OMB Memorandum M-25-21 by making authoritative policy meaning explicit, versioned,
and auditable for high-impact administrative AI.
Rather than relying on probabilistic inference from narrative text, this protocol requires AI
systems to retrieve from authoritative, machine-readable policy memory. Doing so enables
AI outputs to be verified directly against versioned, cryptographically anchored definitions
without inspecting model internals or reconstructing inference. The result is audit-by-
design oversight that reduces downstream reconciliation costs currently borne by CMS,
OMHA, and beneficiary support operations, while allowing industry-led deployment of AI-
assisted care and benefits operations without introducing model-specific regulatory
regimes or expanding federal operational scope.

If useful, I would welcome the opportunity to brief the HHS AI Governance Board or CMMI
staff on how this protocol could be explored through existing sub-regulatory guidance or
voluntary innovation pilots.
