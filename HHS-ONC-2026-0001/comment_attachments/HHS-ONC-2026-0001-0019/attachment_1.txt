Executive Summary: The Case for "Deflationary Infrastructure" The U.S. Healthcare Sector
operates beyond safe margins, with the AAMC projecting a deficit of up to 86,000 physicians by
2036. This submission argues that Artificial Intelligence should be prioritized as a "Deflationary
Infrastructure"—a protective capability designed to preserve the system’s most expensive and
fragile asset: its staffed clinical capacity.
Proposed Doctrinal Standard: We propose that HHS adopt Workforce Readiness Telemetry
(WRT) as a critical interoperability standard.
Definition: Workforce Readiness Telemetry refers to anonymized, aggregate
indicators of operational strain, cognitive load, and staffing fragility. WRT is
intended for system-level planning and readiness assessment and is explicitly not
designed for individual performance evaluation, disciplinary action, or employment
decisions. It is intended to complement existing interoperability frameworks (e.g.,
USCDI, TEFCA), not replace them—focusing on upstream operational inputs that
precede clinical events.
II. Responses to Specific RFI Questions
Question 1: Barriers to Private Sector Innovation and Adoption BLUF: Adoption is throttled
by a "Readiness Bind." Hospitals lack the cognitive bandwidth to adopt tools that do not
immediately reduce operational friction.
● The Gap: HHS currently lacks a standardized metric for "Human Capacity." Staffing is
measured in "FTEs" (Full-Time Equivalents), a lagging indicator that fails to capture

burnout, moral injury, or disengagement. A facility may appear fully staffed on paper, yet
remain operationally degraded due to unmeasured cognitive load.
● The Solution: Prioritize AI that serves as Operational Radar. Tools that predict attrition
30–60 days in advance allow administrators to intervene proactively, reducing the multi-
million dollar annual cost of turnover. Importantly, WRT leverages existing metadata
streams and minimal viable indicators, making it low-burden for frontline systems.
○ Strategic Alignment: This directly operationalizes NHSS 2023-2026 Objective 1.3
("Strengthen the recruitment, retention, and preparedness of the HPH
workforce"), moving workforce retention from a passive goal to an active,
measured capability.
○ Data Support: While nursing turnover costs the average hospital $5.7M annually,
the loss of a single high-acuity physician costs $500,000 to >$1M in replacement
costs alone, consistent with recent AMA and AMN Healthcare analyses.
○ Revenue Impact: For high-revenue specialties (e.g., Neurosurgery), a six-month
vacancy results in >$3M in lost clinical capacity. AI that preserves this capacity
represents a critical form of deflationary infrastructure.
Question 3: Governance and Implementation of Non-Medical Device AI BLUF: Workflow-
embedded AI falls into a "Liability Gap" that chills adoption. Frontline clinicians currently bear a
disproportionate share of the risk for AI-influenced workflows.
● The Gap: FDA regulation covers devices; it does not cover "Algorithm-Augmented
Workflow." In practice, frontline clinicians often shoulder the ambiguity and accountability
for AI-influenced workflows—creating adoption hesitancy in high-stakes care.
● Recommendation: HHS’s internal AI Governance Board should advocate for the
exploration of time-limited federal safe-harbor mechanisms during declared staffing
emergencies. If a clinician follows a federally validated AI protocol during a crisis, they
should enjoy liability protection. This mechanism is essential for deploying AI in high-
stakes environments without paralyzing the workforce.
Question 8: Interoperability to Accelerate AI Adoption BLUF: Interoperability must expand
beyond Clinical Data (USCDI) to include Operational Telemetry.
● The Gap: Current interoperability standards track the patient but ignore the system.
● The Solution: Create a standard for sharing "Aggregate Readiness Signals" (e.g., Bed
Capacity + Staffing Viability).
● Strategic Outcome: This enables Regional and National Surge Prediction. Beyond
daily operations, aggregated telemetry creates a real-time heat map for mass casualty,
pandemic, or geopolitical events (aligning with NDAA Section 735 readiness
requirements). This allows federal planners to load-balance across the national grid
before regional collapse occurs.
● The ONC Role: The ONC is uniquely positioned to define this operational layer in a way
that preserves trust, minimizes burden, and enables downstream innovation, ensuring
that readiness data becomes a national asset rather than a proprietary silo.

Question 10: Prioritized Areas of Research BLUF: Research must validate AI as a tool for
Cognitive Augmentation, not just automation.
● Priority 1: Cognitive Load Mapping. The primary safety endpoint for clinical AI should
be: Does this tool reduce time-to-decision or increase cognitive burden?
● Priority 2: Degraded-State Validation. AI tools must be tested for reliability under
"Degraded Conditions" (e.g., cyber outages, communications latency, mass casualty
surges, or geopolitical conflict). This aligns with CISA’s 2025 Cybersecurity Performance
Goals for critical infrastructure resilience.
Conclusion: The Aviation Parallels Successful high-reliability sectors (Aviation, Energy) do
not use technology to replace the expert operator; they use it to augment situational awareness
and prevent error. Healthcare AI should follow this "Human-System Teaming" doctrine. By
shifting focus from "replacing doctors" to "stabilizing the grid," HHS can transform AI from a
disruptive novelty into a stabilizing component of national health security.
