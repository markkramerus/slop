Accelerating the Adoption of Artificial Intelligence in Clinical Care Subject: Technical Advisory

To the Committee:
I am writing to provide technical feedback on the infrastructure requirements necessary for the
safe acceleration of AI in clinical settings. As a Data Engineering Manager currently overseeing
petabyte-scale genomic pipelines in the life sciences industry (Corteva Agriscience), I manage
the "Data Gravity" and lifecycle challenges of biological AI daily.
While the RFI correctly identifies the potential of AI, it currently overlooks a fundamental
engineering failure mode: Silent Instrument Drift.
1. The Engineering Gap: "Batch Effects" in Production In industrial genomics, we frequently
observe that AI models are highly sensitive to the specific firmware and calibration of the data
capture hardware (e.g., DNA sequencers or imaging arrays). A model trained on data from
"Sequencer A" will often degrade silently when applied to "Sequencer B," interpreting the
hardware noise profile as a biological signal.
In a clinical setting, if a hospital upgrades its MRI firmware or sequencing hardware, an
"accelerated" AI model lacking rigid provenance checks will likely produce diagnostic errors that
are statistically undetectable until patient harm occurs.
2. Recommendation: Mandate Instrument-Agnostic Validation To safely accelerate
adoption, HHS standards must look upstream of the model layer. I recommend that any
framework for AI adoption include a mandatory "Data Engineering Competency" check:
● Metadata Provenance: AI systems must be required to ingest and validate the
hardware metadata (Device ID, Firmware Version) of the input data.
● Drift Normalization: Protocols must be established to distinguish between Biological
Drift (the patient changing) and Instrument Drift (the machine changing).
We have solved this in the private sector by enforcing strict schema contracts on our data lakes.
Clinical systems require the same rigor. Adopting AI without standardizing the underlying data
engineering layer will result in fragile systems that fail at scale.
