The IAC AI Guidance Document serves as a recommendation for IAC-accredited facilities utilizing AI
technology and states the following:
To assure the quality and safety of care delivery when using AI applications for direct-patient care
(clinical*) purposes, each facility should create and follow policies and procedures that address:
1. Training for personnel who use AI
2. Security of AI software, updates, HIPAA considerations, etc.
3. AI for Quality Improvement (if applicable)
4. Appropriate use for each AI application
5. Governance (authority to make decisions regarding AI implementation)
* Clinical use of AI includes image acquisition, image processing/enhancement, image
interpretation, report generation, risk assessment of prognosis, patient history, identification of
critical values/results and equipment quality control.
IAC recognizes that AI use is expanding and there is a lack of uniform processes to assure patient safety and
quality care delivery. Our recently issued guidance represents an interim solution, offering foundational
guidelines to address the most rudimentary quality and safety aspects of clinical AI. However, as cutting-edge AI
innovations continue to evolve, IAC intends to enhance its guidance for the clinical setting.
In this RFI, HHS seeks feedback on ways in which it can best support private sector activities (e.g., accreditation,
certification, industry-driven testing, and credentialing) to promote innovative and effective AI use in clinical
care. IAC's accreditation programs already include thorough assessments of the care environment— including
infrastructure, capacity, systems, and processes— which promote data-driven improvements that lead to better
outcomes. IAC will continue to incorporate established guidelines, including those supporting effective and safe
use of AI, into our accreditation standards.
IAC’s number one priority is to promote safe and equitable patient care, and we stand ready to assist HHS in
ensuring those goals are preserved as AI use in clinical care rapidly evolves. IAC would be happy to meet with
HHS to discuss our work in more detail, as well as a potential future collaboration when guidelines for the use of
AI are established. Please do not hesitate to contact me via email at ttolpegin@intersocietal.org or by phone at
1.800.838.2110. We also offer the IAC website to learn more about our various programs and standards, focused
on the quality of diagnostic imaging and interventional procedures.
