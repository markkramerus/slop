Supplemental Submission to Docket HHS-ONC-2026-0001
Re: Empirical Observations Supporting Deterministic Semantic Memory Framework
Date: February 12, 2026
Original Submission ID: 0010
To the Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology:
Following my prior submission (ID: 0010) addressing agentic drift in non-device AI systems
and proposing the concept of a deterministic semantic substrate for algorithmic
transparency, I respectfully submit this supplemental notice to provide empirical context
supporting that framework.
This supplement accompanies a baseline research report published by the Trust Publishing
Institute (TPI) titled:
The Medicare Digital Visibility & Compliance Shift: 2026–2028
The report documents publicly observable structural shifts in how AI-driven retrieval
systems (including Google AI Overviews, large language models, and AI-enabled browsers)
interpret and represent Medicare plan information. The findings reflect measurable
instability in AI-mediated plan interpretation when authoritative, machine-readable
semantic anchors are absent.
The purpose of this supplemental submission is not to introduce new regulatory
recommendations, but to provide empirical observations that reinforce the architectural
problem described in my original comment: non-device AI systems operating in regulated
benefit environments are prone to interpretive variance when policy meaning is not
embedded in structured, versioned, and retrievable formats.
Key observations documented in the report include:
• Increasing reliance of AI systems on machine-readable plan identity (Plan-ID entity
resolution)
• Measurable interpretive variance in cost-sharing, service areas, and benefit
categorization
• Reduced retrieval instability when structured, fragment-level plan representations
are published
• Reinforcement dynamics in AI-driven discovery environments favoring structured
sources

These findings align with the deterministic semantic substrate model outlined in
Submission ID: 0010, which proposes shifting governance emphasis from model
inspection to content-layer determinism through structured semantic anchoring.
The Trust Publishing Institute (TPI) is a time-bound nonprofit research entity formed to
study structural conditions affecting AI-mediated information stability in regulated
healthcare domains. TPI does not seek designation as a standards authority. Rather, its role
is to document observable system behavior and evaluate architectural characteristics
correlated with improved retrieval stability.
The attached report reflects publicly observable system behavior and does not rely on
proprietary datasets or non-public regulatory materials.
I submit this supplement to ensure that the structural conditions documented in the
Medicare digital ecosystem are included in the administrative record for Docket HHS-ONC-
2026-0001, as they directly inform the governance considerations surrounding non-device
AI.
Respectfully submitted,
