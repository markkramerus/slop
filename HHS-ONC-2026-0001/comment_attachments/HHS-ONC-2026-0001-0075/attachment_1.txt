EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Cellworks Group, a CLIA-certified precision oncology laboratory, submits these comments to
assist HHS in accelerating responsible AI adoption in clinical care. We focus on a critical barrier:
algorithmic services offered lawfully by CLIA-certified laboratories currently lack clear
pathways to obtain Medicare coverage, codes, and payment rates on the same terms as
other laboratory services.
We offer three specific recommendations addressing the RFI's focus areas:
Regulation: Clarify that CLIA oversight combined with rigorous technical assessment provides
sufficient regulatory framework for single-site algorithmic laboratory services, enabling
Medicare coverage without requiring FDA clearance as a prerequisite, while supporting public
trust in diagnostic outputs.
Reimbursement: Endorse the AMA's Clinically Meaningful Algorithmic Analyses (CMAA)
framework to provide predictable coding and payment for algorithmic services, with payment
rates reflecting the substantial resources required to develop and validate these services.
Research & Development: Fund multi-institutional outcomes research to generate real-world
evidence supporting payer coverage decisions for validated algorithmic services, with emphasis
on health equity and access for all Americans.
These recommendations directly address HHS's stated goals: reducing uncertainty that impedes
AI innovation, fostering public trust and confidence in AI tools, aligning federal incentives, and
accelerating access to validated AI tools in clinical care for patients nationwide.

REGULATION: CLARIFY CLIA OVERSIGHT FOR LABORATORY ALGORITHMIC SERVICES
The Regulatory Barrier
Stakeholders face uncertainty about whether algorithmic services require FDA clearance to be
eligible for Medicare coverage. This uncertainty particularly affects CLIA-certified laboratory
services that:
• Are performed exclusively within a single laboratory facility
• Integrate with core laboratory testing (genomic sequencing, molecular analysis)
• Operate under continuous CLIA regulatory oversight
• Are issued under laboratory director responsibility
The High Bar CLIA Services Already Meet
CLIA-certified laboratories offering algorithmic services undergo rigorous oversight:
Analytical Validation: Sensitivity, specificity, reproducibility, bias evaluation
Personnel Standards: Director qualifications, competency assessments, continuing education
Quality Assurance: Proficiency testing, internal quality controls, performance monitoring

Technical Assessment: Many services undergo additional voluntary rigorous technical

• Is recommended in NCCN clinical guidelines
• Has 20+ years of prospective outcomes data
Recommendation
HHS should clarify that CLIA oversight combined with rigorous technical assessment provides
sufficient regulatory framework for single-site laboratory algorithmic services to be eligible for
Medicare coverage and payment. This clarification would:
• Remove unnecessary regulatory uncertainty blocking commercialization
• Recognize that CLIA + rigorous technical assessment establishes rigorous safeguards
• Enable Medicare coverage decisions based on clinical evidence and utility
• Accelerate access to validated algorithmic services
• Foster public confidence in AI tools through transparent governance
Implementation: CMS should issue guidance stating that CLIA-certified laboratory algorithmic
services meeting established rigorous technical assessment standards (e.g., MolDx,NYSDOH or
equivalent) are eligible for Medicare coverage consideration without FDA clearance as a
prerequisite.
REIMBURSEMENT: ENABLE PREDICTABLE CODING AND ADEQUATE PAYMENT
The Reimbursement Barrier
The current coding and payment landscape creates two critical barriers for algorithmic
laboratory services:
1. Coding Uncertainty

• Existing CPT structures (particularly PLA codes) create laboratory-specific codes rather than
function-specific codes
• This prevents multiple validated laboratories from competing on the same code
• Results in 2-3+ year delays as each laboratory seeks unique codes and payer coverage
2. Payment Inadequacy
• Current payment methodologies may not reflect the substantial resources required to:
• Develop and validate algorithmic services
• Conduct prospective outcomes studies
• Maintain ongoing quality surveillance
• Update algorithms based on emerging evidence
The Solution: CMAA Framework
The AMA CPT Panel is developing a Clinically Meaningful Algorithmic Analyses (CMAA)
framework that would:
• Create function-specific (not lab-specific) codes for algorithmic services
• Include a transition pathway for existing PLA-coded services to migrate to function specific
categories.
• Organize codes by clinical evidence depth and utility
• Enable multiple laboratories meeting evidence standards to use the same codes
• Support competitive markets based on quality and outcomes
This framework directly solves the coding bottleneck HHS has identified.
Recommendation
HHS should publicly endorse the AMA CMAA framework and commit to implementation:
1 CMS Endorsement: Issue a statement supporting CMAA as the appropriate coding structure
for clinical algorithmic services
2 Payment Commitment: Commit to Medicare payment implementation by January 1, 2027,

RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT: FUND OUTCOMES INFRASTRUCTURE
The Evidence Gap
While many algorithmic services demonstrate strong analytical and clinical validity, payers
increasingly require real-world outcomes evidence before providing coverage. However:
• Single laboratories cannot fund multi-institutional prospective studies
• Academic medical centers lack incentives to generate comparative effectiveness data
• Payers wait for outcomes evidence before covering services (creating a catch-22)
Recommendation
HHS should establish an "Algorithmic Diagnostics Validation Initiative" to generate outcomes
evidence:
Structure:
• $50-100M annual funding primarily through AHRQ, with NIH coordination
• Competitive grants for multi-institutional outcomes studies
• Focus on algorithmic services with strong preliminary evidence but limited real-world
validation
• Require data sharing to support multiple payers' coverage decisions
Priorities:
• Prospective outcomes studies comparing algorithmic-guided vs. standard-of-care treatment
selection
• Health economics analyses (cost-effectiveness, value-based care implications)

CONCLUSION

benefit.

APPENDIX: SUPPORTING EVIDENCE
CLIA + Rigorous Technical Assessment as Comprehensive Safeguard
Rigorous technical assessment processes (e.g., MolDx) require:
• Analytical Validity: Evidence of accuracy, reproducibility, and performance across populations
• Clinical Validity: Literature demonstrating correlation with clinical outcomes
• Clinical Utility: Evidence that patients benefit when results guide decision-making
• Post-Market Surveillance: Ongoing performance monitoring and outcomes tracking
This framework has successfully validated 200+ laboratory tests with demonstrated clinical
adoption, establishing that rigorous technical assessment combined with CLIA oversight creates
a high bar for quality and patient safety.
Economic Impact of Reimbursement Barriers
Current barriers create:
• 2-3 year delays in payer coverage per algorithmic service
• $5-10M per service in duplicative evidence generation (each payer requires separate
dossiers)
• Venture funding hesitation due to reimbursement uncertainty
• Innovation suppression as companies avoid algorithmic development
CMAA implementation would:
• Reduce coverage timeline from 2-3 years to 12-18 months
• Create predictable coding structure enabling commercial planning
• Support competitive markets (multiple labs using same codes)
• Accelerate innovation by removing reimbursement uncertainty
