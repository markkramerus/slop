Executive Summary
Pair Team is an AI-enabled medical group serving high-needs Medicaid and Medicare beneficiaries. We
operate at the intersection of technology and community health, collaborating with partners like OpenAI,
Anthropic, ElevenLabs, academic leaders such as Dr. Roxana Daneshjou of Stanford University, and over
100 community-based organizations to build an AI care system that automates operations and delivers
proactive support to patients.
Pair Team believes the most efficient and responsible path toward autonomous virtual care begins with AI
serving as the virtual front door and primary coordination layer of care. In this model, AI systems
proactively engage patients, identify clinical and social needs, sequence actions, and escalate to human
clinicians when clinical risk or decision-complexity cross established safety thresholds. This AI-first
approach drives superior patient engagement by providing 24/7 accessibility, multilingual support, and
hyper-personalized outreach that extends care beyond the constraints of traditional, resource-limited
clinics. By expanding when and how patients can access care, AI-enabled engagement improves
prevention and coordination across healthcare and adjacent public programs, alleviates provider
shortages, and reduces total cost of care. However, existing regulatory, reimbursement, legal, and
technical frameworks were not designed for continuously learning, agentic AI. Without targeted
modernization, these structural barriers will continue to delay the transition to autonomous care and limit
the benefits available to patients.
Regulatory Proposals: A Path to Autonomy
In the last year, HHS has taken enormous steps forward that will encourage the implementation of AI
systems in healthcare. Three additional regulatory moves will accelerate action and create a path to
continuous AI advancement:

Expand TEMPO into a Multi-Stage Autonomy Roadmap: The FDA’s TEMPO pilot provides a strong
foundation for modernizing oversight of AI-enabled clinical tools. HHS should encourage the FDA to
utilize the TEMPO pilot to validate a graduated transition to autonomy:
● Stage 1: AI agents operate as recommendation assistants within TEMPO, with higher risk actions
validated by a human provider. Developers track performance, including rates of human
disagreement with AI recommendations on specific tasks.
● Stage 2: Leveraging the performance data and clinical outcomes collected during the pilot, the
AI's scope expands to include the independent initiation of low-risk diagnostic, clinical or
administrative tasks under defined guardrails. For instance, agents may order laboratory tests,
low-risk imaging and medication refills. All such actions would be constrained by established
national clinical frameworks to ensure that AI reinforces evidence-based practice and avoids
generating unnecessary tests, treatments, or procedures with limited clinical benefit.
● Stage 3: As safety and performance are demonstrated at scale, these systems transition into
autonomous orchestrators capable of managing complex, whole-person care workflows, including
more advanced clinical decisions.
Authorize Developer-Led Evaluation Protocols: The FDA has established an important foundation for
iterative improvement through Predetermined Change Control Plans (PCCPs). To support the pace and
complexity of modern clinical reasoning models and digital assistants, this framework could be extended
through a complementary pilot pathway that authorizes developer-led evaluation protocols and
benchmarks. Under this approach, the FDA would review and approve a developer’s evaluation
methodology including performance targets, safety thresholds, and validation procedures, rather than
requiring resubmission for each discrete model change. Once approved, developers could make changes
within the bounds of those validated evaluations, such as incorporating new data types, refining outputs,
or expanding clinical scope, provided performance against the approved benchmarks is maintained or
improved. This approach aligns regulatory oversight with federal investment in shared evaluation
infrastructure, ensuring that AI systems are authorized based on demonstrated performance against
rigorous, real-world benchmarks.
Reduce Legal Uncertainty: As AI systems increasingly function as a virtual workforce, existing legal
frameworks create uncertainty regarding accountability. When clinicians use AI-enabled tools to inform
care decisions, how should responsibility and liability be shared across clinicians, health systems, and
technology developers to ensure safe, effective, and accountable care? To address this, HHS should
articulate a shared-responsibility governance framework for AI-enabled care that clarifies role-specific
expectations and links enforcement action and liability to adherence to core practices, including defining
intended use, ongoing monitoring, human oversight, and transparency to patients.
Reimbursement: Scaling ACCESS-Style Care Management Across
Medicaid and Medicare Advantage
The ACCESS model demonstrates CMS’s leadership in advancing outcome-aligned, engagement-focused
care management under a deflationary pricing scheme. As states and Medicare Advantage plans continue
to evolve their care coordination strategies, ACCESS offers a valuable template that can be extended
across Medicaid and Medicare Advantage through payment, administrative, and technical levers to
support broader adoption. Critically, AI-led care management structures should be tied to deflationary

pricing models by setting rates that require productivity gains from automation and be tied to outcomes
based pricing that deliver actuarially supported total cost of care impact.
Medicaid: Reinforcing AI-Compatible Economics and Accountability
Under existing authorities, CMS can encourage states to adopt ACCESS-like care models by issuing
targeted guidance and embedding expectations directly into state and plan oversight processes. This can
include issuing State Medicaid Director Letters (SMDLs), applying SPA review standards, and
influencing managed care contract design during MCO RFP cycles to encourage adoption of
ACCESS-style payment, accountability, and delivery approaches.
CMS can further accelerate adoption by:
● Encouraging the use of state-directed payments and managed care incentive payments to invest in
ACCESS-like care models and enabling infrastructure.
● Promoting the deployment of AI-enabled agents that access appropriate data sources and interact
directly with beneficiaries to support Medicaid redeterminations and community engagement
requirements as an initial, high-impact opportunity for beneficiary-facing AI.
● Using RHTP as a recurring incentive by tying annual award criteria to the scope and maturity of
AI-enabled care delivery and infrastructure, encouraging states to expand and deepen
ACCESS-like models over time
● Publishing and operationalizing standardized templates such as model MCO contract language,
acceptable outcome measures and reporting expectations, and illustrative payment models
(PMPM, performance-based).
● Creating shared measurement, reconciliation, and payment tools to support consistent
implementation, evaluation, and scaling of ACCESS-like programs across Medicaid managed
care.
Medicare Advantage: Encouraging Adoption Through Incentives and Enablement
While maintaining flexibility for MA plans, CMS can accelerate voluntary adoption of ACCESS-style
models by combining payment incentives with administrative and technical support, including:
● Weighting STARs measures toward ACCESS-aligned clinical outcomes and member experience,
such as control of A1c, blood pressure, and lipids, where continuous AI-enabled care
coordination can measurably improve performance and reduce downstream utilization.
● Incorporating assumptions of increasing efficiency over time into benchmark rate development,
reflecting the role of automation in care coordination.
● Offering technical enablement, such as standardized outcome definitions, benchmarking and
risk-adjustment support, and shared reporting and reconciliation tools to reduce execution
complexity.
By extending ACCESS-style payment, accountability, and operational logic across Medicaid and
Medicare Advantage, CMS can encourage scalable, AI-enabled care coordination while reinforcing
flexibility, consistency, and sustainability across programs.

Technology: National Open-Source Infrastructure
To bridge the gap between general AI capability and clinical reliability, HHS should invest in national,
open-source infrastructure through federal funding to the private sector and academia, creating a shared
foundation for all innovators.
Fund Complex, Real World Benchmarks: Current benchmarks are largely static and single-turn.
Additionally, they focus narrowly on clinical decision-making rather than care delivery more broadly.
HHS should fund the development of evaluation suites that reflect the actual complexity of care:
● Care Planning Benchmarks: Evaluating an agent’s ability to sequence clinical and social actions
(e.g., identifying a rising A1c and simultaneously triggering a transportation benefit).
● Engagement Benchmarks: Shifting from single-turn questions to scaled, multi-turn conversational
benchmarks. These are essential for evaluating if an agent can maintain context over an extended
dialogue to safely and effectively gather information or guide a patient through a complex
process.
Modernize Interoperability for Agentic Access: Safe deployment of agentic AI requires new standards
driven by ONC to ensure accountability for automated actions. We recommend:
● Audit Trails: Requiring all data requests to identify the requester (human, organization, or
automated agent).
● Standardized "On-Behalf-Of" Delegation: Explicitly linking an AI agent’s data access to its
supervising clinician or organization.
● Mandatory Purpose-of-Use Declaration: Using existing HL7 vocabularies to declare the intent of
an automated action at the time of data access.
Conclusion
AI has reached a critical inflection point where it can solve the gaps in access, quality, cost and efficiency
for Medicare and Medicaid beneficiaries that have stymied prior efforts. To realize this potential, HHS
must lead a coordinated modernization of the healthcare ecosystem. By evolving regulatory pathways to
support agentic reasoning, aligning reimbursement with population health outcomes, and investing in
national open-source infrastructure for clinical evaluation, HHS can transform AI from a supportive tool
into a scalable virtual workforce. Pair Team appreciates the opportunity to contribute to this RFI and
welcomes continued partnership with HHS to build a future of radically affordable, high-quality and
accessible care for everyone.
