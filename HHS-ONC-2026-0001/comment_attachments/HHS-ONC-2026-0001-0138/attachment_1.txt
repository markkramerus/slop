Despite the transformative potential of AI-enabled tools to improve cancer care, two systemic
barriers continue to limit equitable adoption across the settings where most Americans receive
treatment.
First, there is a significant disparity in access to advanced digital infrastructure and clinical
decision support across community and rural oncology practices—even though these sites
deliver the majority of U.S. cancer care. Without targeted support, these providers lack the
resources needed to implement, operationalize, and maintain AI-driven technologies that enable
guideline-concordant, precision cancer care.
As a result, patients treated outside major academic centers face uneven access to innovative
tools that could meaningfully influence outcomes. To close this gap, HHS should prioritize rural
and community cancer centers in federal AI adoption programs and ensure these sites have the
technical, operational, and financial support needed to implement and sustain AI solutions
successfully.
Second, to sustain long-term adoption, incentives must be aligned with existing reimbursement
structures and real-world oncology workflows, so that providers are encouraged and financially
able to integrate AI-enabled decision-making support into daily clinical practice over time, not
just during isolated initiatives. Aligning incentives to reflect the ongoing value AI delivers is
essential to drive durable adoption, reduce care variability, and ensure patients benefit from
consistent, evidence-based treatment regardless of geography.
HHS Should Recognize the Critical Role of AI in Equitable Care Access for Community Care
Settings with Targeted Funding and Grants to Community Clinics
Community oncology is where most Americans diagnosed with cancer receive their care, and it
is often more accessible, more cost‑efficient, and closer to patients’ homes compared with
hospital/academic settings. 2,3 Certain populations, however, continue to face obstacles
accessing care, for example, according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network, rural
patients face higher cancer mortality despite lower incidence, and structural barriers.
Additionally, financial barriers, transportation, digital access, and social inequalities limit
equitable outcomes.3 In our experience, community centers have limited resources to adopt
new therapies due to the lack of support for information at the point of care. Additionally, they
lack access to sophisticated technology support teams to help them adopt new AI
technologies. AI-enabled solutions, led by clinical decision and other AI-connected applications,
equip clinicians with the tools required to deliver personalized medicine without manual
research at the point of care and help reduce these disparities.

Recommendation 1: HHS should explicitly recognize community and rural oncology practices as a
priority deployment environment for AI. HHS should direct targeted support, technical assistance,
and matched grants to expand AI‑enabled care‑coordination capabilities, including clinical
decision-making, in independent and network-based community practices, the settings most at risk
of being left behind by uneven AI diffusion and utilization.
HHS Should Partner with CMS to Create Reimbursement Pathways for AI Technologies
AI can streamline documentation, reduce administrative burden, help triage patient care options,
and enhance providers' ability to make evidence-based decisions at the point of care.
Numerous AI tools exist across the oncology workflow to enhance healthcare operations that
face limited resources. The value will come not only from streamlining operations but also from
clinicians' ability to make the best decision for the patient when presented with both evidence
and patient-driven information at the point of care.
The knowledge base of clinical options is rapidly expanding, with novel diagnostics,
therapeutics, and supportive care, to the point that clinicians are becoming overwhelmed by
complexity. AI tools can help reduce this complexity in a timely way, thereby reducing wait times
for care and improving patient satisfaction with care and outcomes.
Currently, Ambient AI scribes are among the fastest‑adopted generative AI tools in health
systems. They are associated with reductions in clinician burnout and cognitive load. These
benefits are especially valuable in oncology, where the burden of both decision-making and
documentation can be high amid the rapid pace of new diagnostic and therapeutic
innovations.4,5,6
However, the high upfront and ongoing costs of AI platforms, integration, and compliance
disproportionately strain community practices operating on thin margins with limited resources
to support implementation and maintenance. Surveys of health systems identify financial
concerns, regulatory uncertainty, and tool immaturity as top barriers to AI adoption.7 Without
aligned incentives, advances will concentrate in large systems, widening disparities and gaps in
care within community care and thereby jeopardizing the timeliness and quality of care received
by the majority of US citizens diagnosed with cancer seen in the community oncology setting.

Conclusion
VieCure stands ready to collaborate with providers, professionals, and governmental
organizations to advance the safe, equitable adoption of AI in clinical care. As HHS considers
strategies to accelerate responsible AI deployment, it is essential that federal policy reflects the
realities of where most cancer patients receive treatment and the structural barriers that limit
access to innovation in those settings.
• To achieve meaningful and equitable progress, HHS should prioritize community and rural
oncology practices for targeted federal support, technical assistance, and grant funding so
these sites have the infrastructure and resources needed to implement and sustain AI-
enabled clinical decision-making and care coordination tools.
• HHS should partner with CMS to establish reimbursement pathways that recognize the
clinical, operational, and patient outcome value of validated, clinician-in-the-loop AI,
including through add-on payments or technology-enabled modifiers within models such as
the EOM.
By clearly prioritizing community oncology and offering incentives that make AI adoption
feasible for practices operating on thin margins, HHS can ensure that AI-enabled precision
medicine is accessible to all patients. By aligning federal investment with the areas of greatest
need and opportunity, HHS can help reduce care variability, expand access to innovative
therapies, and promote guideline-concordant cancer care nationwide.
VieCure appreciates HHS’s leadership and welcomes continued dialogue as these important
policies advance.
