RE: Request for Information (RFI): Accelerating the Adoption and Use of Artificial

Dear Assistant Secretary Keane:
We appreciate the opportunity to respond to this RFI and to share our experience-based feedback
from building and using artificial intelligence (AI) tools as part of clinical care delivery at scale.
Cadence is on the front lines of a new era for healthcare where personalized, technology-enabled
care empowers patients to take control of their health and reduces costs for Medicare, and we
believe our experience is valuable for the Administration to consider. We also appreciate the
“OneHHS” alignment reflected in this RFI and urge the Department of Health and Human Services
(HHS) to use this moment to provide clear, practical policy pathways that enable responsible
scaling of applied AI in real-world clinical workflows.
Cadence delivers Remote Physiologic Monitoring (RPM), Advanced Primary Care Management
(APCM), and Chronic Care Management to nearly 90,000 patients nationwide, a majority of whom
live in rural and underserved communities. AI is already central to this work: we use AI-enabled
tools to scale high-touch coaching, streamline administrative tasks across electronic medical
records (EMRs), and proactively identify patients who may benefit from medication titration –
always with human-in-the-loop clinical oversight. In practice, the promise of “AI in clinical care”
is to enable better clinical operations at scale – summarizing longitudinal trends, prioritizing
patients who need attention, reducing alert fatigue, and embedding recommendations into existing
EMR workflows so clinicians can act quickly and safely.
In response to the RFI’s focus on regulation, reimbursement, and research and development, we
urge HHS to take a pragmatic, pro-innovation approach that:
● Removes outdated payment and program design barriers that discourage the appropriate
use of automation and AI to deliver higher-value care.
● Aligns federal incentives so AI enhances productivity, reduces clinician burden, lowers
costs, and improves outcomes – while protecting patients and maintaining public trust.
● Promotes interoperability and accountable measurement, including timely access to
Medicare claims and standardized, API-based data exchange.

A. Key barriers to innovation and broader adoption of AI in clinical care
Key barriers include Medicare payment models that still equate value with documented minutes
rather than clinical impact. Time-based requirements can unintentionally penalize tools that safely
automate work and expand capacity, discouraging investment in AI-enabled care. Fee-for-service
incentives can also discourage prevention and early intervention – precisely where AI-enabled
monitoring and proactive outreach can produce the greatest downstream savings and improved
outcomes.
Adoption is also constrained by the lack of a reimbursement pathway for the software and
interoperability capabilities that make AI clinically usable. In practice, delivering clinically usable
AI requires continuous engineering and operational investment, such as maintaining integrations
that are not standardized, ensuring secure connectivity for tens of thousands of cellular devices,
and sustaining 24/7 uptime across multiple EMRs and care settings.
Finally, fragmented data and delayed feedback loops slow validation and responsible scaling. Data
remain siloed across EMRs, device platforms, and payers, and outcomes and utilization data –
including claims– are not always available in a timely way. This limits the ability of providers and
vendors to evaluate real-world performance, detect model drift, and iteratively improve tools based
on outcomes and utilization.
B. Regulatory and programmatic actions HHS should prioritize
1. Support a predictable, risk-based posture that distinguishes between AI as a medical
device and AI used for care delivery operations
HHS can accelerate adoption by promoting clear, consistent expectations across divisions and
encouraging a proportionate, risk-based approach. For non-device clinical AI, HHS should publish
practical guidance for governance, auditability, and change management (including when models
are updated) rather than creating uncertainty that chills deployment of low-risk workflow tools.
HHS should also provide clearer boundary guidance for clinical decision support and workflow
tools so developers and health systems can understand when functionality is likely to trigger
medical device oversight versus when it is better addressed through operational governance and
quality management.
2. Enable responsible use of AI while protecting patient information
Trust will depend on strong privacy and security safeguards, clear patient-facing transparency, and
practical guidance on procurement. HHS can support adoption by publishing model contractual
provisions and procurement checklists for AI-enabled services that address data use, security
controls, incident response, model monitoring, and shared accountability between vendors and
providers.

C. Reimbursement and payment policy recommendations
1. Remove time-based barriers that discourage appropriate use of automation and AI in
care management
As we articulated in Cadence’s prior response to the Health Technology Ecosystem Request for
Information (CMS-0042-NC), eliminating or modernizing time-based requirements for team-
based care management services such as RPM would encourage investment in technology that
delivers more personalized and efficient care. Where automation safely reduces the staff time
needed to perform tasks, payment should not penalize adoption of higher-value tools. CMS should
consider payment approaches that recognize engagement and outcomes (not minutes) for
technology-enabled care, paired with guardrails to prevent low-value utilization.
2. Expand access to modern care management models like APCM
APCM is an important step toward proactive longitudinal management that is not artificially
constrained by minute-counting. HHS should remove restrictions that limit APCM to providers
participating in specific value-based models, so more beneficiaries can access technology-enabled,
and specifically AI-enabled, care – particularly in rural and underserved areas. Because APCM
supports flexible team-based care delivery, it is well-suited to integrating AI that improves
productivity and consistency (e.g., intake, summarization, and care gap identification), while
preserving clinician accountability.
3. Modernize reimbursement to recognize the value of software, interoperability, and
AI-enabled clinical support
HHS should create reimbursement mechanisms through both fee-for-service and value-based
pathways that recognize the costs of software capabilities underpinning technology-enabled care,
including interoperability, maintenance, and security. This approach can be paired with guardrails
that raise the bar and protect Medicare from low-value utilization, such as requiring 24/7 clinical
support and meaningful EMR integration so data and actions are visible in the patient’s chart.
4. Enable performance measurement through timely access to Medicare claims and
outcomes data
HHS can promote accountability and public trust by expanding timely access to Medicare claims
data for qualified evaluation and validation of outcomes for AI-enabled clinical care. Claims-based
analyses can help quantify cost and utilization effects year-over-year and may be used to validate
private sector analyses.
D. Interoperability and R&D priorities to accelerate responsible AI
Enhanced interoperability is foundational for clinical AI. HHS should continue to advance
standardized, API-based exchange across settings and vendors, including by improving the
consistency and completeness of longitudinal data available via APIs and supporting
3

benchmarking tools and representative datasets that enable evaluation across diverse populations.
Interoperability policy should prioritize not just data access, but workflow-ready exchange that
supports bi-directional communication (data in, actions out) and reduces custom integration
timelines for providers. In particular, EMR providers should be mandated to provide read/write
API access.
Cadence would welcome the opportunity to partner with HHS on demonstration projects and
applied research that evaluate AI-enabled care management for chronic disease in Medicare
populations, including studies that measure outcomes, clinician burden, and total cost of care when
AI is embedded in real workflows.
