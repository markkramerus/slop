II. Executive Summary
The current healthcare technology ecosystem suffers from systemic failures that prevent true
innovation from flourishing. Digitization through the HITECH Act of 2009 has inadvertently
fostered vendor monopolies and high-cost regulatory hurdles, such as the prohibitive cost of
HIPAA compliance, which stifle the entry of innovative startups.
To restore competition and improve health outcomes, HHS must move beyond strategy to
infrastructure. We propose the creation of a government-backed, "YC-style" AI accelerator that
provides a federal "stamp of approval," backed by a national insurance fund and legal safe
harbors to de-risk startup adoption for large healthcare organizations.
Furthermore, we urge HHS to fund healthcare data exchanges as public utilities, mandate
deterministic governance to ensure auditability, and fix the economic "efficiency penalty" that
currently punishes providers for using AI to work faster.

III. The Innovation Crisis: Monopolies and Compliance Costs
The transition to digital records has failed to create a competitive market, leading instead to
vendor opacity and lock-in where large legacy systems control fragmented data silos.
• The Compliance Barrier: The massive financial burden of HIPAA compliance and
complex regulatory requirements serves as a primary barrier to private-sector innovation.
• The Chilling Effect: Small innovators fear investing in tools that may later be
categorized as regulated medical devices, while institutional legal teams delay adoption
for 12 months or more to avoid perceived liability.
IV. Proposal: A Government-Backed AI Accelerator and Safe
Harbors
To create a level playing field, the government should establish a “Y Combinator-style"
accelerator for healthcare AI startups. This program would act as a trust-bridge through:
• Voluntary Certification: An ONC-administered program to provide a "stamp of
approval" validating safety, interoperability, and bias mitigation.
• National AI Insurance Fund: A government-backed fund, similar to the National
Vaccine Injury Compensation Program, to compensate for rare cases of AI error,
removing the massive liability insurance requirements that block small vendors.
• Legal Safe Harbors: Clear guidance providing legal safe harbors for providers who
implement tools from these government-vetted startups.
V. Economic Alignment: Fixing the "Efficiency Penalty"
Adoption is throttled by the "Efficiency Penalty" in labor-based payment models (RVUs), which
financially penalizes providers if an AI tool allows them to complete a task faster.
• Tiered Reimbursement: HHS should adopt a Tiered Complexity Model: Tier 1 for
administrative software, Tier 2 for assistive pattern recognition, and Tier 3 for high-
complexity predictive modeling.
• AI-Guided Interpretation Codes: CMS should introduce billing codes for "AI-Guided
Clinical Interpretation" to reimburse the cognitive labor of a clinician verifying AI
insights.
VI. Governance & Engineering: Determinism and Auditability
To bridge the trust gap, oversight must shift from statistical confidence to deterministic
safeguards and audit-by-design.
• Deterministic Governance: High-risk clinical tools should be verifiable utilizing
evaluation harnesses that allow changes in models, agent harnesses, and code changes to
produce similar results.

• Agent Trajectory Audit Trails: Systems should generate agent trajectories for every
decision, allowing third-party auditors to reconstruct and "replay" the process bit-for-bit
after an adverse event with.
