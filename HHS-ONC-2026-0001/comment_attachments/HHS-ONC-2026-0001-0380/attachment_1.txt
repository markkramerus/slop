The Orthotic and Prosthetic (“O&P”) Alliance appreciates the opportunity to provide comments
in response to the Department of Health and Human Services’ (“HHS”) Request for Information
on the regulation, reimbursement, and research of artificial intelligence (“AI”) in clinical care.
The O&P Alliance is a coalition of four major national O&P organizations representing over
11,000 certified O&P professionals and over 3,000 accredited O&P facilities that serve millions
of individuals with disabling conditions in need of orthotic braces and prosthetic limbs. The
O&P Alliance is committed to ensuring that individuals with limb loss and limb impairment
receive timely and medically necessary O&P care from qualified practitioners and suppliers.
AI has the potential to improve patient outcomes, expand access to care, and reduce
administrative inefficiencies. However, to realize these benefits, HHS will need to modernize
reimbursement and other regulatory frameworks to enable the adoption of clinically appropriate
AI-tools in O&P care. Also, HHS must also ensure that robust safeguards are in place to prevent
bias in AI systems and prevent the misuse of AI by payers that could restrict access to care or
erode patient protections, with little or no input from real clinicians who treat patients on a daily
basis. This dual approach would help ensure that AI-tools are used to enhance, rather than
replace, clinical judgement and patient-centered care.

HHS Must Adopt a Clinically Appropriate Framework to Support AI Use in O&P Care
AI holds significant potential for advancing O&P care across the full continuum of clinical
services, including pressure mapping, gait analysis, and remote monitoring for long-term
management of patients using prosthetic limbs and orthotic braces. AI-enabled tools can also aid
orthotists and prosthetists in selecting the most appropriate orthosis or prosthesis and reducing
the risk of adverse events (e.g., falls and skin breakdown). When appropriately implemented,
AI-enabled tools can materially improve patient outcomes. In addition, AI-powered tools can
help capture clinician-patient conversations, assist with documentation, and help reduce
administrative burden, which allows orthotists and prosthetists to devote more time to direct
patient care.
Given these benefits, it is critical that HHS adopt a regulatory framework that appropriately
recognizes AI as a potential component of O&P care. Current Medicare and Medicaid
regulations, however, do not adequately account for AI-enabled software and tools. For
instance, there are no Level II Healthcare Common Procedure Coding System (“HCPCS”) codes
that specifically describe or value AI-embedded software or tools in O&P technologies. As a
result, manufacturers who invest in these technologies often cannot achieve the necessary return
on investment due to the lack of reimbursement from Medicare or Medicaid, despite these tools
demonstrably improving patient care. This misalignment between technological advancements
and payment policy creates a barrier to innovation and adoption within the O&P profession, and
limits patient access to clinically beneficial technologies.
Therefore, we urge HHS to address the absence of clear coverage/reimbursement pathways for
AI-enabled tools integrated into O&P devices and technologies. This pathway should address,
for instance:
• Embedded software within orthoses or prostheses;
• AI-driven analytics used for clinical assessment, fitting, and tracking for patients in need
of orthoses or protheses;
• Ambient listening and documentation tools powered by AI to improve the
clinician/patient encounter and clinical workflow; and
• Remote monitoring platforms that support ongoing management of O&P care.
Modernizing regulatory frameworks may require the creation of new HCPCS codes and
reimbursement levels, revisions to established coverage and coding guidance, and alternative
payment mechanisms that recognize the value of AI-enabled tools in O&P care. Where statutory
constraints limit such reforms, HHS should work with Congress to obtain the necessary
authority.

such practices by payers may result in inappropriate denials or delays in care, leading to

Therefore, HHS should ensure that payer use of AI in claims adjudication and utilization
management remains subject to robust transparency and meaningful human oversight, including:
• Requiring that a clinician with relevant qualifications review all denials of claims and
prior authorization requests;
• Requiring payers to clearly inform patients and practitioners when and how AI tools are
used in claims processing or prior authorization decisions;
• Allowing practitioners and patients to opt-out of participation in any program that relies
on AI-enabled tools to render coverage or payment determinations;
• Requiring payers to remain fully responsible for determinations made with AI-enabled
tools and to explain the individualized basis for those decisions;
• Ensuing that patients and practitioners are able to confer with qualified clinicians and
other payer employees with respect to questions regarding prior authorization requests
and claim processing;
• Require payers to conduct internal audits to ensure that any AI-enabled tools comply with
all applicable coverage standards; and
• Require HHS to monitor for discriminatory or biased outcomes that could
disproportionately impact individuals with disabilities or other complex medical
conditions.
HHS Must Develop Polices that Address the Bias
The use of AI raises concerns regarding algorithmic bias and its potential to adversely affect
individuals with disabilities. Many AI tools rely on pattern recognition derived from large
datasets that often inadequately represent individuals with disabilities, including individuals with
limb loss and limb difference. Because O&P patients often present with complex, highly
individualized medical conditions, AI systems trained on generalized datasets may not accurately
capture their unique clinical and functional needs or outcomes. This skewed data can result in
significant bias that adversely impacts individuals with disabilities. Bias can also occur when
AI-tools rely on flawed proxies, such as healthcare costs, to aid in the clinical decision-making
process. For O&P patients, these proxies can underestimate clinical care needs and the long-
term value of certain treatments, which could result in inappropriate restrictions on care.
Accordingly, overreliance of AI, without appropriate safeguards, may ultimately lead to
inappropriate care, delayed care, or denied care.
