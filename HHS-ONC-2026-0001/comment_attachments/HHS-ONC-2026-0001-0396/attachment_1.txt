Vantage Precision Health (VPH) is grateful for the opportunity to submit comments in
response to the Department of Health and Human Services' (HHS) Request for
Information (RFI) regarding the acceleration of artificial intelligence (AI) adoption as part
of clinical care, published in the Federal Register at 90 FR 60108 on December 23,
2025. Vantage Precision Health is a consulting and advisory firm specializing in AI
adoption strategy and program evaluation for behavioral health and aging services
providers. We work directly with community mental health centers, substance use
treatment organizations, psychiatric rehabilitation programs, and providers of long-
term care for older adults the very organizations that would benefit profoundly from
thoughtful AI integration, and that face the greatest risks if that integration is poorly
designed, inadequately evaluated, or prematurely accelerated.
Our perspective is shaped by daily engagement with providers who operate at the
intersection of chronic underfunding, and the collision of growing demand for services
combined with workforce shortages. These providers serve populations people living
with serious mental illness, substance use disorders, co-occurring conditions, and age-
related cognitive and functional decline whose needs are complex, whose
vulnerabilities are significant, and whose voices are too often absent from national
technology policy conversations. We offer these comments in the hope that HHS will
center the experiences of these populations and their providers as it develops its AI
policy framework.
Our Overarching Position
Vantage Precision Health strongly supports the responsible adoption of AI in clinical
care and believes that well-designed, rigorously evaluated AI tools can meaningfully

expand access, reduce administrative burden, improve care coordination, and advance

research infrastructure necessary to ensure that those tools are safe, effective, and
equitable. Acceleration without this infrastructure will not serve patients, providers, or
the public interest.
Behavioral Health and Aging Services: A Distinct Policy Context
Behavioral health and aging services providers occupy a unique and underserved
position in the health information technology landscape. Because these providers were
largely excluded from the Health Information Technology for Economic and Clinical
Health (HITECH) Act of 2009, many lack the electronic health record (EHR)
infrastructure, interoperability capacity, and data governance frameworks that form the
foundation for AI integration. The costs of AI solutions remain prohibitive for many
community-based behavioral health organizations, in part because AI vendors must
build compatibility across dozens of disparate EHR systems a burden that is passed
directly to providers. Sustainable public financing for AI adoption in this sector is not yet
in place, and reimbursement pathways for AI-assisted services remain unclear.
At the same time, the populations served by behavioral health and aging services
providers present distinctive clinical and ethical considerations for AI deployment. The
therapeutic relationship is a primary driver of outcomes in mental health treatment and
the talk-therapy components of addiction treatment, and AI tools that supplant rather
than support that relationship risk undermining care quality. Additionally, older adults,
particularly those with cognitive impairment, may face heightened risks from AI
systems that are not designed with their needs and capacities in mind (similar to what
occurred during the COVID-19 pandemic with many long-term care residents unable to
communicate effectively with family and friends via smartphones or tablets). And the
intersection of AI with sensitive behavioral health data including information
protected under 42 CFR Part 2 raises privacy and confidentiality concerns that
require specific regulatory attention.
Key Recommendations
Uniform standards for AI safety, privacy, and efficacy. HHS should establish uniform
federal standards for AI safety, privacy, and efficacy that are specifically calibrated to
the behavioral health and aging services context. These standards should address the

unique confidentiality requirements of substance use disorder treatment records, the
clinical and ethical dimensions of AI use with populations experiencing serious mental
illness or cognitive decline, and the interoperability gaps that continue to disadvantage
safety-net providers.
Reimbursement pathways for AI tools in BH and AS. HHS should develop and fund
reimbursement pathways that enable behavioral health and aging services providers to
adopt high-value AI tools particularly those that reduce administrative burden,
support care coordination, and enhance clinical decision-making without bearing
unsustainable implementation costs. This should include reimbursement for provider
training and patient education, recognition of the workforce investment required for
responsible AI adoption, and incentives for AI tools that demonstrate measurable
improvements in health-related quality of life rather than engagement metrics alone.
Oversight and funding of outcomes research with healthcare AI tools. HHS should
invest in independent, rigorous outcomes research on AI in behavioral health and aging
services, conducted by researchers without financial ties to AI vendors. The evidence
base for AI in these settings remains nascent especially in behavioral health and the
metrics used by commercial developers e.g., downloads, user engagement, and
customer satisfaction are not adequate substitutes for clinical outcome data. HHS
should prioritize research that examines the impact of AI tools such as therapy
chatbots on recovery, functional status, and quality of life, and that attends to
differential effects across populations defined by race, ethnicity, age, disability status,
and socioeconomic circumstances.
Uniform data and software standards. HHS should oversee and fund the
development of uniform data and software standards for healthcare AI. Nascent tools,
e.g. ambient-listening AI scribes, should be tested using the same representative,
minimally biased data. The AI tools should also be required to be interoperable, as the
lack of this requirement for EHRs and associated databases has produced decades of
difficulty with adoption and use. This has especially affected behavioral health
providers, who were not included in the HITECH Act s funding pool and have lagged far
behind the rest of medicine in the development of EHRs and other specialized
technologies for mental health and substance use disorder treatment.
Engagement of providers and patients in shaping AI data and tool governance. HHS
should ensure that behavioral health and aging services providers, and the patients and
communities they serve, have a meaningful voice in AI governance. This includes
representation on any technical expert panels convened by ASTP/ONC, inclusion in
pilot programs and cooperative agreements, and active outreach to organizations
serving populations that are historically underrepresented in health technology policy.
Non-medical device status. Currently, AI scribes and many other AI tools impacting
clinical care are not recognized as medical devices by the FDA. This classification is
critical for adequate oversight and needs to be changed. In the case of AI scribes,

Conclusion
Vantage Precision Health is committed to advancing the responsible and equitable
adoption of AI in behavioral health and aging services. We believe that HHS has a
historic opportunity to shape an AI policy framework that expands access, improves
outcomes, and builds public trust and we are eager to contribute our expertise to
that effort. We would welcome the opportunity to participate in any technical panels,
listening sessions, or collaborative research initiatives that ASTP/ONC convenes in
connection with this RFI, and we are available to provide additional information or
clarification at any time.
