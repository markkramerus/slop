Summary of recommendations (01, 02, 08, 05, 010)

1. Require continuous evaluation for any federally supported Al implementation.
Make evaluation a non-negotiable component of Al deployment, aligned with the Evidence
Act's intent, with clear expectations for what is measured, how often, and how results are
used to adjust or even pause implementation (21, Q2, 08, O10).

2. Build an evaluation framework that uses EHR and EHR-use metadata.
Use existing EHR metadata (e.9., audit logs, documentation pattems, workflow traces) to
measure how Al affects workflow, team dynamics, and the cognitive environment, rather
than just whether the Alis on (Q1, G2, Q8, Q10). '

3. Tie funding and incentives to demonstrated value, not just use
Link grants, pilots, and payment adjustments to improvements in a small set of meaningful,
feasible, and viable measures (for example, burden reduction, timeliness, care
coordination), rather than to raw Al adoption rates (Q1, Q2, Q10).7

4. Focus on “automation of the obvious, flagging of the not-so-obvious.”
Encourage Al that automates routine, well-understood tasks and surfaces non-obvious risks,
while preserving human expertise and judgment for complex and atypical cases (01, G2,
9, O10).

5. Guard against automation complacency and overload.
Require designs and training that keep humans actively engaged, monitor for over-reliance
and new types of error, and ensure clinicians and staff can operate safely even when Al
systems fail or are unavailable (1, 2, 09, Q10).*

6. Support adoption in resource-constrained and non-tech-hub settings
Pair Al incentives with infrastructure and technical assistance for rural, small, safety-net, and
other resource-limited organizations, so Al does not deepen the divide between large, well-
resourced systems and everyone else (01, O2, 08, 09).

fT. Close the “model-to-practice’ gap for federally funded Al.
Encourage NIH and other HHS funders to support not only model development but also
implementation, evaluation, and operations partnerships, so that fewer Al models end at
publication and more are translated into tools that improve care in practice (01, O2, 08,
O70).
